Clinical Education Hub

A team asked for AI to summarize documents. Research showed the real problem was finding and using the information they already had, so I built a searchable knowledge platform first. Now I'm leading its AI roadmap.

The requestWhat I was asked for

Marketing wanted AI-generated summaries of dense clinical studies so the sales team could get through them faster.

The real problemWhat research showed

I interviewed 25+ reps, ran field surveys, audited the ShowPad content library, and sat in on sales training. The content was accurate. Reps just couldn't find it, couldn't save it, and couldn't learn from it in the formats it came in.

That made it a retrieval and application problem, not a summarization problem. More AI on top of hard-to-find content would not have fixed it.

What I shippedScope and tradeoffs

A searchable knowledge platform, designed and built in Next.js in a 4-day sprint:

  • Structured summaries that cite their sources
  • Audio versions for people on the road
  • Illustrated snapshots and flashcards for different learning styles

I deliberately left AI out of the MVP until the core experience was validated with users, so AI would build on content people already trusted.

The outcomeWhat happened next

Leadership selected the Hub for company-wide rollout to 4,000+ sales and marketing employees, and turned my internship project into a contract product-owner role.

The AI roadmapWhat I own now

I own requirements and the roadmap, aligning marketing, compliance, and IT on content ownership, review, and platform integration. The phased AI plan adds semantic search and a citation-grounded evidence assistant that declines to answer when the evidence is thin. Success is measured by time-to-evidence and citation accuracy.

How I work

Three habits, each learned on a real project.

Diagnose before building

The Hub started as a request for AI summaries. Interviews showed the problem was finding and using evidence, which changed what we built.

Ship the smallest thing that proves it

A 4-day MVP without AI earned the trust, and the company-wide rollout, to build AI features next.

Trust is a feature

One confident wrong answer can make people stop using an AI tool. I design assistants to cite sources and decline when evidence is thin, and I measure accuracy, not just usage.

Why I build

I started in healthcare as a scribe, medical assistant, and patient care tech, and watched software get in the way of the people it was meant to help. That gap between powerful technology and the people who rely on it is the problem I want to work on, in any industry.

I bring user research, enough technical depth to build (Next.js, and AI/ML study at UT Austin), and the go-to-market skills to explain why a product matters.
